Carl Gustav Jung and William Moulton Marston are among the
 most widely known of the psychologists who’ve increased the
 understanding of personality. Jung’s ideas formed the basis of
 the Myers-Briggs Type Indicator assessment, while Marston
 was responsible for developing the DISC model.
Current theories are grouped into the following categories:
vr Psychodynamic
vr‘ Behavioral
vw Situation or social driven patterns
vv Existential (or patterns to make meaning of life)
As you may imagine, plenty of researchers and writers have
 talked about these points of view, which are rich and complex
 theories. Many of the theories address two core qualities of
 personality:
vr Sources within the person that drive efforts to control or
 be part of his environment (degrees of assertiveness)
vr How much social connection or engagement individuals
 typically display (degrees of expressiveness)
Depending on your take of the qualities and how they blend, a
 host of models exist to give greater refinements to these twin
 dimensions of personality.
Observers and researchers of personality also have explored
 the roles of family history, genetic traits, and learning in the
 development of typical personality patterns and not so typical
 patterns — or what’s categorized as dysfunctional personalities.
 For example, everyone is motivated by self-interest, but the nar-
 cissist goes too far in self-promotion and self-indulgence.

 can access thorough information on all the theories of per-

 roots of personality gives you an appreciation for the different
 ways of seeing personality patterns.
