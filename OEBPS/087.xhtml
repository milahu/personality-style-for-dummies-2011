Differences of Style Should
 Be Appreciated
Sometimes you may forget to thank the Direct person for
 taking charge and initiating change or the Considerate person
 for having a calm demeanor when the argument seemed to be
 getting tense.
Take time to notice the differences you see in others, thank
 people for what they’ve said or done, and especially thank
 folks when what they have done is something that doesn’t
 come naturally for you or them.
Mixing Styles Can Compensate
 for Blind Spots on a Team
Competitive sports teams often comprise a high number of
 Direct personality styles, which makes sense when you think
 of the core characteristics of the style (for more on the Direct
 Style, see Chapters 1 and 2).
Work teams, though, benefit from having a greater range of
 styles. Diverse styles help the team compensate for possible
 blind spots. The careful Considerate serves as a buffer to the
 impatient Direct; Directs help push things through when the
 Systematic prefers one more look at things. Each style helps
 balance out the other.
When expanding or replacing a member of your team, it’s
 worth taking into account the spread of styles, and, if it makes
 sense to do so, consider people with styles that complement
 what you already have.
