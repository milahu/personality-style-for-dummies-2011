Familiarizing Yourself with the
Basics of Personality Style

Personality has been a subject of interest from the very begin-
nings of civilization. The earliest notions of personality sug-
gested that individuals were born with ways of being in the
world (Navajo). The Greek physician Hippocrates (460-370
BC) codified these “ways of being” into a medical theory
based on temperaments — certain innate human moods,
emotions, and behaviors. Modern psychology has provided
numerous other empirically framed theories of personality.

Knowing What Makes Up a Style

You have a preferred personality style that developed over time.
Your style reflects how you like to work — alone or with others —
how you spend your time — talking or reading — and how you
deal with and share information. Your style is essentially the way
you act when you're able to do things your own way.

Your preferred style is likely to be used most of the time —
that’s why it’s “preferred”; however, you may decide to
behave differently from your style from time to time. You're
likely to be more effective when you capitalize on the benefits
of your style and also flex or adapt your style to account for
the style of the person with whom you wish to interact.

Your style is determined by the degree of assertiveness and
expressiveness you demonstrate. Assertiveness is essentially
the effort that you make to influence or control the thoughts
and actions of others. Expressiveness is the effort you make to
control emotions and feelings when relating to others.
