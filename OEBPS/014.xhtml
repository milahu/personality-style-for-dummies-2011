The Direct Style is based on being assertive and controlled
 in expressing your emotions. Direct Style is high on assertive
 and low on expressive behaviors. For more on where this
 style lands on the style continuum, see Chapter 1.
Direct Style has the following characteristics:
vr Take charge
vw Get results — now!
vr Compete with vigor
vw Organize tasks for practical outcomes
 vr Pursue risks confidently
Individuals of the Direct Style walk fast, speak fast, and assert
 control.
Noticing the trouble spots
 of the Direct Style
All styles bring benefits and potential trouble spots. The trou-
 ble spots can begin to outweigh the perceived value of the
 style unless the individual learns how to moderate the style
 and flex.
Flexing your style means adjusting your instinctual behavior
 to achieve the desired result. If you don’t flex, you're likely
 to experience frustration and disappointment in many situa-
 tions, as will those around you. See the section “Making the
 Direct Style flex” for more information.
The Direct Style isn’t without trouble spots. Often those who
 prefer the Direct Style are unaware that their behaviors trig-
 ger reactions that can create problems. Some of the trouble
 spots include the following:
vr Being perceived as a poor listener
vr Appearing impatient and terse with others
