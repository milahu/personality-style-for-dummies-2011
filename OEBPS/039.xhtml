Your personality style greatly influences how you do this and
 is represented in how you build trust, encourage actions, and
 celebrate the results of team performance. In this section, you
 see how the four styles can be useful in team situations, and
 conversely, how they can create challenges.
Direct Style
The Direct Style works best when the team needs direction
 and team members want someone to take charge. This style
 says, “I know what needs to be done and here’s how we
 should do it!”
A few benefits of this style as a team member include
 vr Communicates assertively by asking difficult questions
and raising tough issues
vr Forward thinker and initiator of a course of action and
 decision-making processes
vr Provides candid, decisive opinions on achieving a final
 outcome
You may also notice a few trouble spots:
 vr Appearing overly critical, which interferes with establish-
 ing rapport and closes down group discussions
vr Seeming impatient and too open to taking risks, which
 interferes with mutual planning and problem-solving
vr Displaying assertive behaviors, which leaves some of the
 other team members feeling unsupported and discouraged
This style doesn’t work well when team members are cautious
 and like to stick with established, safe processes.
Spirited Style
The Spirited Style works best when team members are open
 to exploring new options and being spontaneous. This style
 says, “I have some great ideas. What if we . . . or considered...
 and maybe looked at . . ..”
