Flexing requires you to assess the needs of the interaction
 and to shift (even momentarily) in your choice of a response.
 You need to determine how much assertiveness and emo-
 tional expressiveness is required of you in an interaction.
Some of the common flexing challenges for the four leading
 styles are as follows:
vw Direct Style leaders can fail to recognize that a follower’s
 feelings have been hurt by a direct comment or that a fol-
 lower wants to move at a different pace. Knowing when
 to increase emotional expressiveness and tone down
 assertiveness benefits those with this leading style.
w Spirited Style leaders can fail to recognize when followers
 want a concrete plan and want to know that the details
 are important to the leader. Knowing when to moderate
 both assertiveness and emotional expressiveness helps
 those with this leading style.
vw Systematic Style leaders can fail to recognize that a fol-
 lower wants more brainstorming of possibilities and
 greater enthusiasm about working together. Knowing
 when to elevate both assertiveness and emotional
 expressiveness benefits those with this leading style.
vr Considerate Style leaders can fail to recognize that a
 follower wants more directness and more attention to
 developing a plan to balance out the focus on empa-
 thetic interactions. Knowing when to elevate an assertive
 response and decrease emotional expressiveness ben-
 efits those with this leading style.
Tactics to use to enhance your awareness of when to flex
 include:
vr Ask followers if more or less assertiveness would benefit
 the working relationship with a question such as “Would
 you like for me to be more direct?”
vr Ask followers if more or less emotional expressiveness
 would benefit the working relationship with a question
 such as “How can I be more supportive of your efforts?”
vr Monitor the time you spend “telling” versus “asking” in
 an interaction. If you haven't heard from your followers
 in the last 60 seconds, you're talking too much.
