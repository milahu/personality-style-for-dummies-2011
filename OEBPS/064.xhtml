Of course, the most important determinant of the coach-
ing process is the fit of coaching style and knowledge of the
 needs of the coachee. If a Considerate Style coach attempts
 to work with a Direct Style coachee and neither knows how to
 flex, then the coaching experience is likely to be short lived.
Flexing requires you to assess the needs of the interaction
 and to shift (even momentarily) in your choice of a response.
 You need to determine how much assertiveness and emo-
 tional expressiveness is required of you in an interaction.
Some of the common flexing challenges for the four coaching
 styles are related to shifting degrees of assertiveness and
 expressiveness as follows:
=” Direct Style coaches can fail to recognize when a
 coachee’s feelings have been hurt by a direct comment
 or that a coachee wants to move at a different pace.
 Knowing when to increase some emotional expressive-
 ness and tone down assertiveness benefits those with
 this coaching style.
w Spirited Style coaches can fail to recognize when the
 coachee wants a concrete plan and needs to know that
 the details are important to the coach. Knowing when
 to moderate both assertiveness (around ideas) and
 emotional expressiveness benefits those with this
 coaching style.
vr Systematic Style coaches can fail to recognize that a
 coachee wants more brainstorming of possibilities and
 greater enthusiasm about the relationship. Knowing when
 to elevate both assertiveness and emotional expressive-
