 A team is more than a working discussion or problem-solving
 group. Teams go through stages of development and require
 the fulfillment of various roles and methods to produce synergy
 and collaborative efforts. In all phases of teaming, interacting
 with team members means that personality styles play a role
in how well the team really works together. Communicating,
 problem-solving, planning, managing expertise, and addressing
 conflict are a few of the processes of teams where the outcome
 is dramatically affected by personality styles.
Because teaming situations often require working extended
 periods of time with individuals across the organization,
 differences in perspective, strategy, and “how to get things
 done” are magnified by personality style differences. The
 team member who walks into the meeting room and immedi-
 ately puts an agenda on the wall is engaging differently from
 the team member who walks in, talks with everyone, and gives
 handshakes and back slaps while telling stories about last
 weekend’s game. Without understanding differences, these
 two could easily interfere with getting to the task at hand
